# Discussion

This study extends the defence system analysis of *Acinetobacter* spp. (Muthuraman et al., 2026) to the full ESKAPE pathogen panel and tests whether the RESTRICT/FACILITATE dichotomy identified in that genus reflects a genus-specific evolutionary history or a cross-genus principle of bacterial defence architecture. The central findings are threefold. First, defence system repertoire encodes substantial species-level signal, with a Random Forest classifier achieving a balanced accuracy of 0.874 [0.852-0.895] under phylogenetically grouped cross-validation, compared to a stratified null of 0.167. Second, RM-mediated restriction of ARG accumulation generalises beyond *Acinetobacter*, with defence profiles significantly predicting high-ARG burden in three of six ESKAPE species and confirmatory SHAP-direction restriction signals identified in five species-by-ARG-class cells spanning both Gram-negative and Gram-positive taxa. Third, this generalisation is partial. The SspBCDE-facilitated ARG accumulation characterised as a defining feature of *A. baumannii* IC2 clones is not recapitulated in other ESKAPE members, and species-specific systems, not RM or SspBCDE, dominate classification of *E. faecium* and *P. aeruginosa*. Taken together, these results support a model in which RM-mediated restriction represents a broadly conserved but species-modulated axis of defence-MGE coevolution [CITE: defence systems embedded in or co-evolving with MGEs; Payne et al. 2021 or equivalent], while the facilitative arm of the RESTRICT/FACILITATE dichotomy reflects lineage-specific evolutionary history rather than a universal mechanism.

The high balanced accuracy of defence system-based species classification reflects a fundamental distinction between Gram-positive and Gram-negative defence architectures [CITE: Gram-positive vs Gram-negative defence system repertoire comparison; Bernheim and Sorek 2020 or equivalent]. *S. aureus* and *E. faecium*, the two Gram-positive ESKAPE species, were classified with near-perfect recall (0.993 and 0.953, respectively), consistent with their defence repertoires lacking the Type I and Type IV RM systems prevalent across Gram-negative genomes and instead encoding predominantly Type II RM and abortive infection systems [CITE: RM type distribution by Gram stain: Type I/IV Gram-negative, Type II Gram-positive; Abi systems in Gram-positive bacteria]. In *E. faecium*, AbiH was the top-ranked SHAP driver for the EF class. The statistical equivalence between Logistic Regression and Random Forest (McNemar p = 0.494) carries a biological implication. Logistic regression classifies by weighting individual features independently; Random Forest can additionally capture combinations, for example the joint signal of RM-present and SspBCDE-absent simultaneously. Because both models perform identically, those combinatorial patterns contribute little beyond individual features alone. In practical terms, ESKAPE species differ in which defence systems they carry as individual markers, not in synergistic or epistatic pairings of systems. *A. baumannii* was the single species with substantially reduced recall (0.700), a consequence of IC2 clonal lineage dominance, as defence gene contraction in IC2 clones produces predominantly SspBCDE-only profiles that generate near-featureless feature vectors, partially overlapping with other species at low total defence counts. The C3 holdout resolves this ambiguity. When tested on 33 published *A. baumannii* genomes drawn from lineages beyond IC2 (Muthuraman et al., 2026), AB recall rose to 0.939. That the same model, trained on the same features, recovers high AB recall when applied to non-IC2 lineages confirms that the cross-validated 0.700 reflects IC2-specific information loss rather than a fundamental ceiling on defence system features as AB discriminators.

Defence system profiles predicted ARG burden significantly in three of six species, all Gram-negative: *E. cloacae* complex (RF AUROC = 0.853), *K. pneumoniae* (RF AUROC = 0.896), and *P. aeruginosa* (RF AUROC = 0.722; Results §2). The three non-significant species each failed for distinct reasons. For *A. baumannii*, within-species ARG variance is clonally structured by IC2 dominance, and the signal that Q2 depends on is simply absent in this dataset. This is a technical limitation of the cohort composition, not evidence that defence systems are irrelevant to ARG acquisition in *A. baumannii* broadly. For *S. aureus*, BA = 0.514 and AUROC = 0.566 are genuinely near-chance, as no SA-filtered defence feature produced consistent SHAP directionality, and the SA result is a biological negative at this sample size. *E. faecium* presents a third pattern. The global Q2 test was non-significant (BA = 0.489, p_adj = 0.120), yet Test B identified significant RM restriction signals in two mechanism-class-specific cells: EF/macrolide-MLSB (AUROC = 0.743, p_adj = 0.049) and EF/tetracycline (AUROC = 0.814, p_adj = 0.048), both carrying negative SHAP for RM Type IIG and Type II respectively. This dissociation indicates that RM restriction in *E. faecium* is class-specific, detectable when ARG burden is restricted to plasmid-mediated resistance classes [CITE: macrolide-MLSB and tetracycline as predominantly plasmid-mediated resistance classes in Enterococcus] but diluted below detection when all ARG classes are pooled under a global burden label. That the restriction signal is carried by RM Type IIG and Type II rather than the Type I subtype most prominent in the published *Acinetobacter* analysis is consistent with the known dominance of Type II RM in Gram-positive genomes [CITE: Type II RM prevalence in Gram-positive bacteria] and extends the RESTRICT principle to Gram-positive ESKAPE members, to our knowledge for the first time [VERIFY: search prior literature on RM restriction of ARG burden in Gram-positive ESKAPE species; if a prior report exists, soften to "consistent with" rather than "for the first time"].

ESKAPE defence profiles, once phylogenetically dereplicated, do not resolve into discrete archetypes. The apparent ARI of 0.383 between the K=10 K-means partition and species labels in the full 878-genome dataset was attributable to IC2 clonal density. Repeating the analysis on 95 phylogroup representatives collapsed ARI to -0.004, and the gap statistic returned K=1 as optimal for the dereplicated dataset. The instability between K-means and hierarchical Ward linkage at K=10 (ARI=0.468) confirmed that no stable partition was recoverable regardless of algorithm. The biological interpretation is that ESKAPE defence repertoires form a continuum shaped by cumulative horizontal acquisition rather than convergent assembly into stable modules. This is consistent with the defence island model, in which individual defence systems are acquired and lost as discrete HGT events [CITE: defence islands and horizontal acquisition; Doron et al. 2018 or equivalent], but the combined repertoire across genomes varies continuously with genome size, ecological history, and phage pressure [CITE: defence system diversity shaped by phage pressure and ecological context; Bernheim and Sorek 2020 or equivalent]. The implication for the RESTRICT/FACILITATE framework is that it operates at the level of individual system types rather than whole-genome phenotypes. A genome does not belong categorically to a RESTRICTOR or FACILITATOR archetype, but expresses restriction or facilitation through the presence or absence of specific systems within a continuous repertoire landscape.

Two pre-registered sensitivity analyses refined the primary Q2 interpretation. Test A established that binary RM Type I presence is the operative encoding for ARG burden prediction. Substituting copy number for binary presence degraded AUROC in all three significant species, most strongly in *P. aeruginosa* (delta = -0.142), despite the copy-number feature ranking first or third by SHAP in PA and EC respectively. The degradation under higher SHAP rank indicates that copy number introduces noise relative to the binary threshold. RM presence represents a qualitative gate on incoming DNA rather than a dosage-dependent mechanism, consistent with the observation that a single RM system is sufficient to restrict the majority of incompatible phage and plasmid DNA [CITE: RM restriction efficiency and single-copy sufficiency; Vasu and Nagaraja 2013 or equivalent]. Test B extended Q2 to ten ARG mechanism classes and identified seven significant cells across eight passing the 30/30 sample floor. Three cells confirmed canonical RM restriction by SHAP direction: KP/aminoglycoside (RM Type II, mean signed SHAP = -0.0039), EF/macrolide-MLSB (RM Type IIG, -0.0052), and EF/tetracycline (RM Type II, -0.0040), all resistance classes predominantly carried on conjugative plasmids that RM directly gates [CITE: aminoglycoside resistance plasmid carriage in K. pneumoniae; macrolide and tetracycline resistance plasmid-mediated in Enterococcus]. The remaining four significant cells (three beta-lactam and KP/sulfonamide) showed RM SHAP values near zero. For beta-lactam, the most probable explanation is that ESBL genes, notably CTX-M variants, are frequently mobilised by IS element transposition rather than conjugative plasmid transfer alone [CITE: CTX-M mobilization by ISEcp1 and related IS elements], a mechanism that does not require full plasmid entry and is therefore not subject to canonical RM gating; prediction in these cells is likely driven by other defence features or ecological co-variation between clinical beta-lactam burden and niche-specific defence composition. The quinolone negative control failed the 30/30 floor in all six species, consistent with the near-binary chromosomal distribution of quinolone resistance across ESKAPE and validating the pre-specified test design.

Interpretation is limited by several design characteristics, each acknowledged in the pre-analysis plan and addressed where possible. Phylogroup count was low for *E. faecium* (7 phylogroups) and *S. aureus* (9 phylogroups), reducing statistical resolution for both species and making bootstrap confidence intervals anticonservatively narrow. For these two species, the BA significance test rather than the AUROC point estimate was designated the operative criterion before analysis, a pre-specified response to the small phylogroup problem. ARG burden was operationalised as a within-species binary tertile label by deliberate design, since a global burden label would confound ARG count with genome size across six ESKAPE species of substantially different sizes. The trade-off is that the label pools all resistance mechanisms, whether plasmid-mediated or chromosomal, under a single burden rank. Defence system annotations reflect the characterised bacterial immune system catalogue at the time of analysis. Because this catalogue is still actively expanding [CITE: recent review of bacterial defence system diversity and discovery], reported feature matrices represent conservative lower bounds on true repertoire rather than exhaustive profiles. Mash-based grouped cross-validation is not a formal mixed-model population structure correction, though the grouping was validated by MLST concordance across 109 sequence types, with 99.1% of all multi-genome STs assigned to a single phylogroup. Finally, ML models were restricted to defence system presence/absence features per the pre-registered analysis plan. HMRG, MGE, and IS element data were collected but not included, and whether incorporating these features reveals niche-specific genome architecture beyond defence composition alone is an open question for subsequent work.

This study demonstrates that RM-mediated restriction of ARG accumulation is not a property unique to *Acinetobacter* but a conserved principle extending across Gram-negative and Gram-positive ESKAPE pathogens. The FACILITATE arm of the RESTRICT/FACILITATE dichotomy reflects lineage-specific evolutionary history. SspBCDE-mediated facilitation is an IC2 clonal characteristic, not a broadly applicable mechanism, and no equivalent facilitative system was identified in the other five ESKAPE members. These findings reframe the dichotomy from a single-genus observation to a cross-genus framework in which the restriction component is generalisable and the facilitation component requires species-by-species investigation. Extending this framework to incorporate HMRG, MGE, and IS element co-variation alongside defence composition represents the natural next step toward a comprehensive model of defence-mediated genome plasticity in bacterial pathogens.

SHAP TreeExplainer attribution of the primary RF Q1 model produced a global ranking dominated by features absent from the published *Acinetobacter* analysis. This is expected rather than contradictory. The systems reported in Muthuraman et al. (2026) were identified by Fisher's exact test on within-*Acinetobacter* prevalence variation; features that vary most within one species are not necessarily those that best discriminate six species against each other. Global SHAP rankings reflect cross-species discriminatory power, and these two objectives select for different features. That all four published systems with significant pairwise associations (RM, SspBCDE, Gao_Qat, PD-T4-5) nonetheless appeared in the ESKAPE global SHAP top 30 confirms that the published signal generalises partially across genera, as these systems carry cross-genus discriminatory information even when they are not the primary classifiers at the ESKAPE scale. The SHAP direction for SspBCDE was positive for AB classification, consistent with IC2 clonal dominance in the AB training class rather than with any causal link between SspBCDE and *A. baumannii* identity; the negative RM direction in the AB class reproduces the negative co-occurrence reported in Muthuraman et al. (2026; log2 OR = -6.09) across all 878 ESKAPE genomes. For non-AB species, the primary SHAP drivers were species-specific systems not characterised in the published analysis: AbiH for *E. faecium* [CITE: AbiH characterisation in Gram-positive bacteria or Enterococcus; verify prior reports] and CRISPR-Cas for *P. aeruginosa*, the latter consistent with the documented high prevalence of functional CRISPR-Cas systems in clinical PA isolates [CITE: CRISPR-Cas prevalence in clinical P. aeruginosa]. These species-specific drivers indicate that the RESTRICT/FACILITATE framework, while generalisable at the RM system-type level, does not account for the full defence variation that distinguishes ESKAPE species from one another.
